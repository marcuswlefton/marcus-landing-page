One connected view

Before you try harder,
look at what is asking more of you.

An illustrative situation

“My most important work
happens after dinner.”

The signal
Your energy is good in the morning. Routine approvals take those hours. The priority waits.
The first test
Give one recurring decision a clear owner, standard, and escalation rule. Protect the recovered time for the priority.
What to watch
Does the important work move earlier—while decision quality holds? If it doesn’t, revisit the explanation.

Six dimensions.
One useful place to start.

Clear ownership → fewer approvals → capacity for the work that matters.

Why start here?

The symptom is evening work. The clue is that good-energy hours are being consumed by decisions that may not need you. That makes ownership a useful first test.

If low energy persisted during protected time, capacity would deserve attention. If delegated decisions created rework, capability or the standard might be the constraint. The starting point changes with the evidence.

This is an illustrative hypothesis, not a diagnosis or a promised result. The aim is better performance with less avoidable personal cost.
