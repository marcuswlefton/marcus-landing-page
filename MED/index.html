The Minimum Effective Dose.

The inputs that stack the odds in your favor.

There will always be another protocol, supplement, wearable, diet, productivity system, recovery tool, or optimization to try. Most are downstream.

Before assuming you need more, inspect the conditions you're repeatedly asking yourself to function under.

Light. Sleep. Food. Movement. Attention. Stress. People. Environment. Meaning.

Don't mistake a temporary state for an identity.
Inspect the inputs first.

Rhythm

Does your system know when to be on and when to be off?

Circadian rhythm sits upstream of sleep, alertness, metabolism, recovery, and cognition.

You don't need to perfect sleep. You need enough consistency that your biology isn't constantly guessing what time it is.

  • Get outside and get daylight after waking.
  • Get regular daylight throughout the day.
  • Keep sleep and wake timing reasonably consistent.
  • Protect enough opportunity to sleep.
  • Make nighttime darker, quieter, and less stimulating.
Rhythm before optimization.
FOR THE NEXT 3 DAYS 3 days

Anchor the clock.

  • Get outside shortly after waking.
  • Keep wake time inside the same 30–60 minute window.
  • Reduce unnecessary bright light before bed.
Notice: waking ease, morning alertness, afternoon energy, sleepiness at night, sleep onset.
02 / 07

Fuel

Are you supplying what the system needs?

Food provides the energy and raw materials your brain and body use to function, repair, regulate, and adapt.

That doesn't mean chasing individual nutrients or trying to “hack dopamine.” It means your inputs deserve intention.

Think in five variables
  • Quality: mostly nutrient-dense, minimally processed foods with enough variety.
  • Quantity: enough total energy and protein for your goals and demands.
  • Timing: support training, cognition, digestion, energy, and sleep.
  • Hydration: match fluids and electrolytes to activity, environment, and losses.
  • Response: pay attention to what actually happens in you.
Best practices are starting points. Your response determines what stays.
FIELD TEST 3 days

Improve the first two inputs.

Make your first two substantial meals mostly whole foods, include a meaningful protein source, eat enough for your demands, and hydrate consistently.

Notice: energy stability, hunger, cravings, mental clarity, training, digestion, sleep.
A broader definition of diet
Your diet is more than what you eat. What you watch. What you listen to. What you scroll. Who you spend time with. The environments you occupy. Be intentional about what enters the system.
03 / 07

Capacity

Can your body comfortably handle the demand?

Build more physical capacity than your normal life requires.

  • Strength and muscle.
  • Aerobic fitness.
  • Regular movement.
  • Enough usable mobility for what you actually want to do.

You don't need to live in the gym. Physical reserve matters whether you're competing professionally, running a company, raising a family, or simply trying to remain capable as you age.

Reserve is not excess. It is the space between what life asks of you and what your system can handle.
TRY THIS TODAY Today

Change state through the body.

Take a brisk 20–30 minute walk, complete a short strength session, or break up prolonged sitting with brief movement bouts.

Before / after: rate energy, mood, mental clarity, and physical tension.
04 / 07

Attention

What are you repeatedly giving your mind to?

Attention is an investment. Some inputs compound. Others simply consume.

Invest

Deep work. Skill acquisition. Creation. Relationships. Deliberate practice. Solving meaningful problems.

Spend carefully

Reactive feeds. Constant novelty. Unnecessary switching. Notifications. Meaningless consumption.

Reduce unnecessary cognitive load. Subtract before adding. Protect periods of directed attention.

Match challenge to skill

Too little challenge and attention drifts. Too much and you can tip into overload. Near the edge of your current capability is often where deeper engagement becomes possible.

Flow becomes more likely when you combine clear goals, immediate feedback, deep concentration, and challenge–skill balance.

You cannot command flow. You can design conditions that make it more likely.

Leave room for boredom

Not every unstimulated moment needs to be filled. Sometimes the system needs enough space to stop consuming, disengage, wander, integrate, and reset.

Focus is useful only when aimed at the right thing.
60-MINUTE TEST 45–90 min

One task. Then no input.

  • Choose one meaningful task.
  • Phone away. Notifications off.
  • Set a clear finish line.
  • Make the challenge demanding enough to require you.
  • Then take 10 minutes with no phone, podcast, or work.
Notice: urge to switch, challenge level, depth of focus, and how the no-input period feels.
05 / 07

Regulation

Can you recognize your state — and change it when necessary?

There is no single optimal state. Different demands require different levels of activation.

The skill is knowing: What state am I in? What state does this task require? Can I move the needle?

Notice breathing, tension, energy, fatigue, attention, emotion, hunger, and cognitive quality. Then learn what reliably shifts you: movement, breathing, music, light, environment, social interaction, stillness, food, or rest.

Stress → Recover → Adapt

Performance is not sustained maximal intensity. Apply enough stress to create adaptation. Then disengage enough to absorb it. Sprint. Recover. Repeat.

A 60–90 minute focused block can be a useful starting experiment. It is not a biological commandment. The better question is whether you can recognize when quality starts to fall.

Know what actual rest is

Switching from work to scrolling is not necessarily recovery. Sometimes recovery means reducing input altogether.

High performance isn't being calm. It's being able to access the state the moment requires.
STATE CHECK 2–5 min

Move your state on purpose.

Before an important task, rate arousal from 1–10. Decide what level would serve the task better. Use one lever — breathing, movement, music, light, or stillness — and rate again.

Notice: could you identify your starting state? Could you move it? Did the new state improve performance?
06 / 07

Environment + Connection

What around you is making the desired state easier or harder?

Willpower is expensive. Environment changes the default.

Look at your physical space, digital environment, calendar, cues, friction, access, and people. Make desirable behaviors easier. Make undesirable behaviors harder.

Social fitness

Maintain relationships that provide connection, support, challenge, belonging, play, and perspective.

Humans regulate each other. The people and environments around you influence your state, behavior, and perception of what is normal.

Design the conditions, not just the behavior.
TODAY 15 min

Reduce friction + connect.

Make one environmental change that makes a desired behavior easier. Then have one 15-minute undistracted conversation with someone you care about.

Notice: did the environment reduce the need for willpower? Did the conversation shift mood, energy, or perspective?
07 / 07

Direction

What is all this capacity being used for?

Performance without direction is just efficient movement.

Know what matters. Know what you're moving toward. Then reverse-engineer that into today's behavior.

Values → North Star → Current Season → Today's Actions

Beliefs shape behavior

Your beliefs influence what you notice, attempt, persist through, and interpret as possible. Treat them as hypotheses, not facts.

Growth is not pretending every outcome is possible. It's refusing to let an untested belief unnecessarily shrink the field of action.

Leave room for play

Not everything needs to improve a metric. Do things because they're interesting, fun, connecting, or because you enjoy being alive while doing them.

Purpose gives direction. Play makes the system worth sustaining.
END-OF-DAY CHECK Today

Make the day mean something.

Write the one outcome that would make today feel meaningfully well-used. Spend your first available focused block moving it forward.

Notice: does clarity reduce procrastination? Where does your calendar contradict what you say matters?
The Subtraction Experiment

Before adding more, remove one thing.

Remove one recurring input that may be costing more than it gives. Test the absence before adding another habit, protocol, or tool.

  • Notifications
  • A feed
  • Late caffeine
  • Background content
  • A low-value meeting
  • A redundant protocol

Then ask:

What changed?
Was the change meaningful?
What is the smallest dose that seems sufficient?
Is this worth keeping, adjusting, or removing?

Everything Else Is Conditional

Make every intervention earn admission.

Supplements, wearables, fasting, recovery tools, and advanced interventions can all be useful. But first ask:

What problem does this solve?
Do I actually have that problem?
Is it limiting me now?
Is there a simpler solution?
How will I know if it worked?
